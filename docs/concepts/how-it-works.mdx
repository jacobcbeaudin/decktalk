`decktalk build` turns a markdown script and HTML pages into one mp4 in six stages. To build your
first video, follow the [Quickstart](/quickstart).

You write four files, and DeckTalk writes everything else.

1. `script.md` holds what the voice says, where each `## N.` heading starts section N.
2. `decktalk.toml` maps each section to a page and a scene, or to a clip of your own video.
3. `cues.json` gives every moment the page declares the spoken phrase it lands on.
4. The page, such as `deck/index.html`, holds the scenes and their slides.

## The six stages

Each stage reads what the stages before it wrote. `decktalk build` runs all six in order, and each
one is also a command of its own with the same name.

| Stage | Command | Reads | Writes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `narrate` | `decktalk narrate` | `script.md` | `build/narrate/takes.json`, one take and one words file per section, and `build/narrate/narration.mp3` |
| `cue` | `decktalk cue` | `build/narrate/takes.json` | `build/cue-times.json` |
| `record` | `decktalk record` | `build/cue-times.json` | `build/recordings/` |
| `soundscape` | `decktalk soundscape` | `build/narrate/takes.json` | `build/soundscape/` |
| `assemble` | `decktalk assemble` | `build/narrate/takes.json`, `build/recordings/`, `build/soundscape/` | `build/sections/`, then `build/final/` |
| `verify` | `decktalk verify` | `build/cue-times.json`, `build/final/` | nothing |

- `narrate` turns the script into one spoken take per section and a start and an end for every word
  in it. A take is found by content alone, so renumbering a section or retitling one voices nothing.
- `cue` turns each cue phrase in `cues.json` into a second on its own section's clock.
- `record` records each page section in headless Chromium, playing the page against those seconds.
- `soundscape` generates the music, the ambience bed and the effects the project describes.
- `assemble` cuts each section to its span, joins them, mixes the whole soundtrack and encodes one
  film, with captions, chapters, a cut list, a transcript page and a poster beside it.
- `verify` measures the finished film: every section start, every cut, every seam and every landing.

[The CLI reference](/reference/cli) lists every command with its options and its result.

## Why the order is what it is

The order is not a preference. Each stage needs something the stage before it wrote, and the two
stages that can spend money sit as late as they can.

- `narrate` is first because the word clock is what every later measurement is made against. A
  second means nothing until the voice has said the words it counts from.
- `cue` is second because a phrase becomes a second only once there are words with times on them.
- `record` is third because the recorder plays the page to those seconds, so the picture lands on
  its word before anything is cut.
- `soundscape` is fourth because it is the last stage that can buy anything. Running it after
  `record` means that `decktalk build --to record` stops before it. A draft loop of
  `decktalk build --no-voice --to record` therefore spends nothing and still exercises the cues, the
  pages, the recording and the checks.
- `assemble` is fifth because it consumes what all four stages before it wrote. It needs the take
  index for the lengths, the recordings for the picture, the soundscape for the mix and the narration
  for the voice.
- `verify` is last because it measures the film, and there is no film until `assemble` has written
  one.

`--from STAGE` and `--to STAGE` run a span of the six, and `--skip STAGE` runs every stage but one.
`decktalk assemble --skip soundscape` mixes the narration and the clip audio and leaves the generated
sound out.

## What judges, and what produces

Two commands exist to be run before the pipeline, and neither one makes a film.

`decktalk check` judges `script.md`, `cues.json` and the pages, and prices what a voiced build would
cost. It produces nothing a viewer receives, so an agent that wants the price of a run makes this one
call. `check --no-pages` judges the written files with no browser at all and says which judgements it
could not reach. `check --no-frames` keeps the browser and drops the freeze comparison.
`check --fix` applies every safe fix, and an unsafe fix is printed either way and never applied.

`decktalk storyboard` freezes every slide at every cue onto one page, `build/storyboard.html`. It is
the human checkpoint before voice credits are spent, because it draws what the film will show in the
order it will show it. Five selectors narrow the sheet: `--section`, `--slide`, `--after`, `--before`
and `--at`.

## Why the cuts are exact

Every reveal starts on its word because DeckTalk finds narration t=0 inside every recording, to the
frame.

| Risk | What DeckTalk does |
|---|---|
| A browser starts to record at a moment nobody chose. Chromium on Windows starts later than on Linux. | The recorder covers the page in magenta from the first paint, and the first clean frame after the cover is narration t=0. |
| Chromium sends a frame only when the page paints. | The cover carries a 2 pixel square in the bottom-right corner that turns once every half second at 3 percent opacity, so frames keep coming under the cover and through the recording. |
| t=0 is known only to the nearest frame. | The recorder captures at 25 frames a second, which is not a setting, so t=0 is exact to one frame of 40 milliseconds. |
| No cover is found at all. | `record` takes the first painted frame plus `record.settle_seconds`, and failing that `record.fallback_first_paint_seconds` plus the settle, and it says in both cases that the start was a guess. |
| Section lengths add up to fractions of a frame. | `assemble` cuts each section to whole frames counted from the start of the narration. |

`record` looks for the cover only in the first `record.cover_scan_seconds` of each recording, which
is 4 seconds by default. Nothing here depends on wall-clock time.
[The handshake](/concepts/page-contract#the-handshake) shows each wait the recorder makes.

## The build without voice

`--no-voice` gives every section a placeholder take instead of a spoken one, so the run needs no
API key and buys nothing.

- The placeholder is a click track with a soft click at the start of every estimated word and one
  where the last word ends, at -24 dBFS.
- `narrate` spaces the estimated words at `narration.silent_words_per_minute`, which is 150 by
  default, and adds each declared pause, beat, lead and tail.
- Every other stage runs as usual, so a build without voice exercises the cues, the pages, the
  recording, the cut and the checks.
- `cue` marks a section whose words are estimated, because its seconds are placeholders too.
- The clicks are what lets `verify` compare the picture with the sound, so `CUE_OFF` still reports a
  reveal that misses its word.
- `assemble` skips the loudness pass, because a click track has no programme loudness worth matching
  and normalising it would move the clicks the check listens for.

A voiced run is the same command without `--no-voice`. On a terminal it prices the spend and asks
before it buys, and without a terminal it refuses unless `--spend` or `--no-voice` is passed.
`--max-cost N` refuses before the first call when the most the run can cost is over N dollars.

## Caching

A stage whose inputs have not changed is skipped, so an edit to one sentence rebuilds one section.

`narrate` names each take by a hash of the content that produced it, which is the section's words
and every `[voice]` key that changes how it is read. A section whose hash has not moved plays the
take it already has. `narration.cache_dir` puts the take files outside `build/`, where a fresh clone
and a second worktree find them again.

`record` keys each recording on the URL it would open, which already carries the scene, the resolved
cues and the spoken words with their seconds, on the frame geometry, the colour scheme and the motion
the render asks for, and on what the page puts on screen for that one section. That last part is the
markup of the scene the section plays, the rest of the page file that every scene shares, and the
content of every project file the page loaded. So an edit inside one scene records the sections that
play it and leaves the other sections of that page alone, an edit to the page's head or its styles
records every section of it, and swapping one picture for another records the sections that show it
although no line of HTML changed.

`--section N` records section N whether it changed or not, and `--force` builds again from nothing
while keeping every voiced take. [Rebuild one section](/guides/rebuild-one-section) says what each
edit runs again.
