first video, follow the [Quickstart](/quickstart).

A project is four files, and DeckTalk writes everything else.

1. `script.md` holds what the voice says, where each `## N.` heading starts section N.
2. `decktalk.toml` maps each section to a page and a scene, or to a clip of your own video.
3. `cues.json` gives every moment the page declares the spoken phrase it lands on.
4. The page, such as `deck/index.html`, holds the scenes and their slides.

## The six stages

Each stage reads what the stages before it wrote. `decktalk build` runs all six in order, and each
one is also a command of its own with the same name.

| Stage | Command | Reads | Writes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `narrate` | `decktalk narrate` | `script.md` | `build/narrate/takes.json`, one take and one words file per turn, and `build/narrate/narration.mp3` |
| `cue` | `decktalk cue` | `build/narrate/takes.json`, and `build/recordings/` when it is there | `build/cue-times.json` |
| `record` | `decktalk record` | `build/narrate/takes.json`, `build/cue-times.json` | `build/recordings/` |
| `score` | `decktalk score` | `[score]` in `decktalk.toml` | `score/`, the bought sounds and their ledger, and `build/score/` |
| `assemble` | `decktalk assemble` | `build/narrate/takes.json`, `build/cue-times.json`, `build/recordings/`, `score/`, `build/score/` | `build/sections/`, then `build/final/` |
| `verify` | `decktalk verify` | `build/narrate/takes.json`, `build/cue-times.json`, `build/recordings/`, `build/final/` | nothing |

- `narrate` turns the script into one spoken take per turn, the stretch of a section one voice reads,
  and a start and an end for every word in it, then joins a section's turns on the section's clock. A
  take is found by content alone, so renumbering a section, retitling one or renaming a voice voices
  nothing.
- `cue` turns each cue phrase in `cues.json` into a second on its own section's clock.
- `record` records each page section in headless Chromium, playing the page against those seconds.
- `score` generates the music, the ambience bed and the effects the project describes.
- `assemble` cuts each section to its span, joins them, mixes the whole soundtrack and encodes one
  film, with captions, chapters, the placements, a transcript page and a poster beside it.
- `verify` measures the finished film: every section start, every cut, every seam and every landing.

[The CLI reference](/reference/cli) lists every command with its options and its result.

## Why the order is what it is

The order is not a preference. Each stage needs something the stage before it wrote, and the two
stages that can spend money sit as late as they can.

- `narrate` is first because the words are what every later measurement is made against. A
  second means nothing until the voice has said the words it counts from.
- `cue` is second because a phrase becomes a second only once there are words with times on them.
- `record` is third because the recorder plays the page to those seconds, so the picture lands on
  its word before anything is cut.
- `score` is fourth because it is the last stage that can buy anything. Running it after
  `record` means that `decktalk build --to record` stops before it. A draft loop of
  `decktalk build --no-spend --to record` therefore spends nothing and still exercises the cues, the
  pages, the recording and the checks.
- `assemble` is fifth because it consumes what all four stages before it wrote. It needs the take
  index for the lengths, the cue times for the sounds on their cues, the recordings for the picture,
  the score for the mix and the narration for the voice.
- `verify` is last because it measures the film, and there is no film until `assemble` has written
  one.

`--from STAGE` and `--to STAGE` run a span of the six, and `--skip STAGE` runs every stage but one.
`decktalk assemble --skip score` mixes the narration and the clip audio and leaves the generated
sound out.

## What judges, and what produces

Two commands exist to be run before the pipeline, and neither one makes a film.

`decktalk check` judges `script.md`, `cues.json` and the pages, and prices the narration a voiced
build would buy. It produces nothing a viewer receives, so an agent that wants the price of the
voice makes this one call, and `decktalk score --no-spend` prices the sound. `check --no-pages`
judges the written files with no browser at all and says which judgements it could not reach.
`check --no-frames` keeps the browser and drops the freeze comparison. `check --fix` applies every
safe fix, and an unsafe fix is printed either way and never applied.

`decktalk storyboard` freezes every slide at every cue onto one page, `build/storyboard.html`. It is
the human checkpoint before anything is bought, because it draws what the film will show in the
order it will show it. Five selectors narrow the sheet: `--section`, `--slide`, `--after`, `--before`
and `--at`.

## Why the cuts are exact

Every reveal starts on its word because the recorder decides what second each frame shows, rather
than finding out afterwards when a browser happened to paint it.

| Risk | What DeckTalk does |
|---|---|
| A browser starts to record at a moment nobody chose, and Chromium on Windows starts later than on Linux. | The recorder holds the page clock still once the page has loaded and painted, so frame 0 is second 0 and there is no start to find. |
| A browser paints when it can, so a busy frame lands later than an idle one. | For each frame `k` the recorder moves the page's timers on by one frame, steps the section clock to `k / fps` and captures what the page shows, so every frame is the page at its own second. |
| Something animates between two frames. | The runtime holds every CSS animation, transition and Web Animation at the second since its cue was due, and calls every `DeckTalk.frame` hook with the frame's second. |
| A heavy page takes longer to draw than one frame lasts. | The recorder waits for each frame, so a heavy page records slower than real time and never wrongly. |
| Section lengths add up to fractions of a frame. | `assemble` cuts each section to whole frames counted from the start of the narration. |

The recorder steps at `video.fps`, and nothing here depends on wall-clock time.
[The handshake](/concepts/page-contract#the-handshake) shows each wait the recorder makes, and
[the decision note](/decisions/the-recorder-steps-the-clock) says why time belongs to the recorder.

## The build that does not spend

`--no-spend` plays every take already on disk, voiced or placeholder, and gives each section whose take
is missing a placeholder instead of a spoken one, so the run needs no API key and buys nothing. Spend
gates money and nothing else, so a free provider such as `dtsp`, which speaks through a server on this machine, still makes
each missing take once a voice is named. The
voice is built only when a take must be made, so a project that commits its takes and its
`[voices.narrator] id` builds its real film on a machine that holds no key and no environment at all. Each section that plays a placeholder is
reported once as [`TAKE_MISSING`](/reference/findings/TAKE_MISSING), with the command that buys its
take. It is a warning, so the default threshold still builds the film.

- The placeholder is click audio with a soft click at the start of every estimated word and one
  where the last word ends, at -24 dBFS.
- `narrate` spaces the estimated words at `narration.placeholder_words_per_minute`, which is 150 by
  default, and adds each declared pause, beat, lead and tail.
- Every other stage runs as usual, so a build that does not spend exercises the cues, the pages, the
  recording, the encode and the checks.
- `cue` marks a section whose words are estimated, because its seconds are placeholders too.
- The clicks are what lets `verify` compare the picture with the sound, so `CUE_OFF` still reports a
  reveal that misses its word.
- A sound the score describes and has not bought plays as silence, reported once as
  [`SOUND_MISSING`](/reference/findings/SOUND_MISSING). It is a warning too, so the film is still
  made.
- `assemble` skips the loudness pass, because a placeholder has no programme loudness worth matching
  and normalising it would move the clicks the check listens for.

A run that buys is the same command with `--spend`.
[What spends and what does not](/requirements#what-spends-and-what-does-not) says when a run with
neither flag asks, and [the ceiling](/requirements#the-ceiling) is how `--max-cost` refuses one.

## Caching

A stage whose inputs have not changed is skipped, so an edit to one sentence rebuilds one section.

`narrate` names each take by a digest of the content that produced it: the turn's words, the
voice in force, its model and output format, `[voices.NAME] speed` and the provider's own voice fields,
such as `[elevenlabs] stability`. A turn whose digest has not moved plays the
take it already has. Every take a voice spoke lives in the takes directory, `takes/` inside the
project, which you commit, so a fresh clone finds them. `narration.store_dir` is the take store, a
second copy of every take this machine bought, which every project looks in second.

`record` keys each recording on the URL it would open, which already carries the scene, the resolved
cues and the spoken words with their seconds, on the frame geometry, the colour scheme and the motion
the render asks for, and on what the page puts on screen for that one section. That last part is the
markup of the scene the section plays, the rest of the page file that every scene shares, and the
content of every project file the page loaded. So an edit inside one scene records the sections that
play it and leaves the other sections of that page alone, an edit to the page's head or its styles
records every section of it, and swapping one picture for another records the sections that show it
although no line of HTML changed.

`--section N` records section N whether it changed or not. A build that finds nothing moved keeps
the film and its measurement, and reports `assemble` and `verify` as `kept`. `--force` builds again
from nothing, measures again, and keeps every voiced take and every bought sound. [Rebuild one
section](/guides/rebuild-one-section) says what each edit runs again.
