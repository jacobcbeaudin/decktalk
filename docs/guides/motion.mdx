---
title: Things that move
description: Move an element from one word to another, turn a loop, draw every frame from the section second, play a film in a pane, or hand a section's picture to a program, and let verify measure each of them.
---

A moment is a reveal. It is over in 0.46 seconds at most, and verify measures it as one step in the
picture at its cue. Anything longer is a **motion**, the other noun the page contract has. A motion
may run for any length, it is declared on the element it moves, and its length is the seconds between
two cues rather than a number in the markup, so a line voiced again times it again.

DeckTalk asks for every thing that moves to be declared, and refuses motion it was not told about.
Verify reads a reveal as a rise in how much of the frame changed, so anything moving beside the
reveal rises too and reads as the reveal starting early. A declared thing is masked out of every cue
it overlaps, and a motion that starts or stops on a cue is measured on its own instead.

`decktalk init --example motion my-motion` writes a project that does every kind of motion on this
page. Its first section moves a packet across the frame, turns a ring and springs a card in. Its
second fills a bar from script. Its third is drawn by a Python script rather than a page. Its fourth
plays a film with its own sound in a pane, made at build time by another short script.

Those two scripts are programs the project names, and a machine refuses a project's programs until
you allow them, because a program runs with your user's rights and can read every file you can, your
`.env` included. Read `render/draw_bars` and `render/make_film`, then build the example with them
allowed for that one run:

```console
decktalk build --set record.programs=run
```

[Programs a project names](/requirements#programs-a-project-names) says how to allow them on a
machine instead.

## Choose the noun

| You want | Write | What verify does with it |
|---|---|---|
| An element arrives, steps back, comes forward or leaves on a word. | A moment: `data-in`, `data-back`, `data-front` or `data-out`. | Measures the step at its cue. |
| An element travels from one word to another. | A motion: `data-motion` with `data-motion-from` and `data-motion-to`. | Masks it, and measures its departure and its arrival on its own lane. |
| Something turns for as long as its slide is on screen. | A loop: `data-loop`. | Masks it, and measures no landing. |
| Script draws the progress of a motion between two words. | `DeckTalk.motion(el, { from, to, ease, draw })`. | The same as a motion in markup. |
| Script draws every frame from the section second, such as a canvas. | A frame hook: `DeckTalk.frame(fn, { region })`. | Masks its region. |
| A program other than the page draws the whole section. | A render section: `render_command` on the `[[section]]`. | Masks and measures the landings the program writes, or measures none of the section's cues without them. |
| Footage with its own sound plays inside a page, framed and drawn over, while the voice keeps going. | A pane clip: `data-clip` with `data-clip-from`. | Masks the pane from its cue, measures its departure on the cue, and measures the clip's own landings on the film's clock. [Play a clip](/guides/clip-section#a-clip-in-a-pane) covers it. |
| Footage you already have plays full frame with its own sound, and the voice pauses. | A clip section: `clip` on the `[[section]]`. | Checks its start and its seams. [Play a clip](/guides/clip-section) covers it. |

A `<video>` or an `<audio>` element on a page is
[`PAGE_REALTIME_MEDIA`](/reference/findings/PAGE_REALTIME_MEDIA). It plays at its own rate rather
than on the stepped clock, so the recording could not say which frame of it shows at which second.
Footage belongs in a pane with `data-clip`, which the recorder draws frame by frame on the stepped
clock, or in a clip section, and a moving picture a program makes belongs in a render section.

## The four eases

`data-motion-ease`, and `ease` in `DeckTalk.motion`, names how a motion moves between its two cues.

| Ease | How it moves | Its arrival |
|---|---|---|
| `firm` | Fast out and slowing in, with a little speed kept into its last frame. It is the default. | An edge: the motion stops dead on one frame. |
| `glide` | At one speed from departure to arrival. | An edge. |
| `settle` | Fast out and slowing until it is barely moving. | At rest: it slows to nothing, with no frame where it stops. |
| `spring` | A critically damped spring stretched over the motion, within a thousandth of rest at its arrival and with no bounce. | At rest. |

`firm` is the default because an arrival has to stop on a frame edge to be measured. Verify finds an
arrival as the last frame on which the motion still moved, and a motion that keeps some speed to the
end has a last moving frame that sits exactly on its cue. A motion that eases to rest moves less and
less, so its last measurable frame comes before its cue by however long the slow tail is, and a
viewer sees it settle before the word.

So a `settle` or `spring` arrival is allowed only on a cue that `cues.json` marks `"verify": false`.
Anywhere else, `decktalk check` raises
[`MOTION_EASE_UNMEASURABLE`](/reference/findings/MOTION_EASE_UNMEASURABLE) before anything is
recorded. A departure is measured whatever the ease, because every ease leaves its first frame
moving.

## A motion in markup

A motion names the `@keyframes` it moves through, the cue it departs on and the cue it arrives on.
Both are cue names local to the slide, like a moment's, and both join the slide's cues, so each needs
its row in `cues.json`. This is the packet in the example's first section.

{/* input: src/decktalk/template/examples/motion/deck/index.html, slide 1.1 */}
```html deck/index.html
<style>
  .packet { left: 120px; top: 352px; width: 140px; height: 140px; border-radius: 20px; background: var(--ink); }
  @keyframes travel { from { transform: translateX(0px) } to { transform: translateX(1540px) } }
</style>

<div class="packet" data-motion="travel" data-motion-from="go" data-motion-to="now" data-motion-ease="firm"
     data-describe=""></div>
<div class="label" data-in="now" data-describe="a label that says it stopped on its word">on its word</div>
```

{/* input: src/decktalk/template/examples/motion/cues.json, section 1 */}
```json cues.json
{ "id": "1.1:go", "phrase": "when I say go", "occurrence": 1 },
{ "id": "1.1:now", "phrase": "one word now", "occurrence": 1 }
```

The packet leaves on "go" and stops on "now", and the runtime plays `travel` for exactly the seconds
between the two. Nothing in the markup is a length, so rewriting the sentence between the two words
makes the packet travel faster or slower and still land on "now".

Until its departure the element rests as its own style draws it, so the first step of its
`@keyframes` should be that same style. A first step that differs makes the element jump on the
departure frame.

A motion whose arrival is not after its departure, or whose cue has no second, never plays, and the
page reports it as [`PAGE_MOTION_CUE_UNKNOWN`](/reference/findings/PAGE_MOTION_CUE_UNKNOWN).

The label beside the packet is an ordinary `data-in` on the same cue the packet arrives on. Verify
measures it as a reveal with the packet masked out, which is what keeps the cue measured as a step.

## A spring that comes to rest

The card in the same slide springs in from "spring" and comes to rest on "rest". A spring has no
edge to measure, so the arrival cue opts out of verify.

{/* input: src/decktalk/template/examples/motion/deck/index.html and cues.json, slide 1.1 */}
```html deck/index.html
<div class="card" data-motion="slide-in" data-motion-from="spring" data-motion-to="rest" data-motion-ease="spring"
     data-describe="">A card on a spring</div>
```

```json cues.json
{ "id": "1.1:spring", "phrase": "on a spring", "occurrence": 1 },
{ "id": "1.1:rest", "phrase": "without a bounce", "occurrence": 1, "verify": false }
```

Its departure on "spring" is still measured, and only its arrival is left out.

## A loop

A loop names the `@keyframes` it turns through and how long one turn takes. `data-loop-seconds` runs
from 0.2 to 60 and is 1 when it is left out.

{/* input: src/decktalk/template/examples/motion/deck/index.html, slide 1.1 */}
```html deck/index.html
<style>
  .ring { left: 1700px; top: 80px; width: 96px; height: 96px; border: 10px solid var(--accent); border-right-color: transparent; border-radius: 50%; }
  @keyframes spin { to { transform: rotate(360deg) } }
</style>

<div class="ring" data-loop="spin" data-loop-seconds="6" data-describe=""></div>
```

A loop starts turning when its slide mounts and turns until the slide leaves. It names no cue, so it
is never a landing, and verify masks the whole box it sweeps out of every cue on its slide.

## A motion drawn from script

`DeckTalk.motion(el, { from, to, ease, draw })` is the same noun for a page that draws the motion
itself. `from` and `to` are cue ids, `ease` is one of the four eases and is `firm` when it is left
out, and `el` is the element, or a selector for it, that the motion draws inside.

`draw(progress, seconds)` is called on every frame between the two cues, with the eased progress
from 0 to 1 and the section second. Nothing is drawn before the departure, and the last call is made
exactly at the arrival with progress 1. This is the bar in the example's second section.

Nothing is drawn after the arrival, so `draw` finishes by then. Just before its last call, `draw` is
called once more with progress 1 at half a second past the arrival, and when the element shows
something different there, such as a fade timed from the second once the progress reached 1, the page
reports [`PAGE_MOTION_UNFINISHED`](/reference/findings/PAGE_MOTION_UNFINISHED) at the arrival's cue:
the picture stops where the arrival left it. Move the arrival to a later cue, or end what `draw`
changes by the arrival.

{/* input: src/decktalk/template/examples/motion/deck/index.html, slide 2.1 */}
```html deck/index.html
<template data-slide="2.1" data-preview-seconds="8" data-owns="fill finish" data-describe="a bar that fills between two words">
  <div class="track"><i></i></div>
</template>

<script>
  DeckTalk.on("2.1:fill", (slide) => {
    const bar = slide.querySelector(".track i");
    DeckTalk.motion(bar, { from: "2.1:fill", to: "2.1:finish", ease: "glide", draw: (p) => { bar.style.width = `${p * 100}%`; } });
  });
</script>
```

No attribute in the markup names "fill" or "finish", so the slide lists them in `data-owns`. That is
how a slide owns a cue only a script serves.

A cue the page does not know, or an arrival that is not after its departure, is
[`PAGE_MOTION_CUE_UNKNOWN`](/reference/findings/PAGE_MOTION_CUE_UNKNOWN), and the motion draws
nothing.

## A frame hook and its region

`DeckTalk.frame(fn, { region })` calls `fn(t)` on every frame with the section second, for
something that is drawn rather than animated: a canvas, a typed line, a scrolling voice line.
`region` is the element, or a selector for it, that the hook draws inside, and verify masks that box
out of every cue while it is on the page.

A hook with no region is [`PAGE_MOTION_UNDECLARED`](/reference/findings/PAGE_MOTION_UNDECLARED),
because nothing tells verify where it draws. A hook is also how the recorder knows a frame changed:
it writes the frame before again for a frame where nothing it knows of moved, so anything a page
draws outside the DOM, a cue, an animation or a declared motion is drawn from a hook. A page that
holds a canvas is captured on every frame regardless. [Still
frames](/decisions/the-recorder-steps-the-clock#still-frames) lists what counts. A hook that throws is reported once as
[`PAGE_FRAME_THREW`](/reference/findings/PAGE_FRAME_THREW) and is not called again.

This voice line scrolls under a fixed playhead at the speed of speech. It is drawn from the second
alone, so a recorded frame and a preview frame of the same second are the same picture.

{/* input: tests/e2e/landing/deck/index.html, cut to its shape */}
```html deck/index.html
<canvas id="voice" width="1920" height="300"></canvas>

<script>
  const PX_PER_SECOND = 340, HEAD = 960;
  let ctx = null;
  DeckTalk.frame((t) => {
    ctx = ctx || document.getElementById("voice")?.getContext("2d");
    if (!ctx) return;
    ctx.clearRect(0, 0, 1920, 300);
    for (const word of DeckTalk.words) {
      ctx.fillText(word.word, HEAD + (word.at - t) * PX_PER_SECOND, 260);
    }
  }, { region: "#voice" });
</script>
```

The pictures above the voice line still arrive with `data-in` on their words, and verify measures
each of them with the voice line masked out.

## A composition page

A composition page is a page section whose page draws the whole picture itself, in a canvas, in
WebGL or with three.js. It is an ordinary page section, and it reads its timing from the runtime
rather than keeping a clock.

| Member | What it holds |
|---|---|
| `DeckTalk.cues` | Every cue the page plays, keyed by cue id, against its second on the section clock. |
| `DeckTalk.words` | Every word the section speaks, as `{ word, at, voice, turn }`. Each word is lower case with its punctuation taken out, which is how the runtime matches it, and `voice` and `turn` say who says it, so a dialogue can place each voice's words on a side of its own without naming anyone. |
| `DeckTalk.frame(fn, { region })` | The hook that draws each frame from its second, inside the region it names. |

Key every change on a cue's second from `DeckTalk.cues` rather than on a number of your own, so a
line voiced again moves the composition with it. A cue that only the composition uses still needs a
slide that owns it, through `data-owns` or a script slide's `owns`.

A WebGL canvas drawn from a frame hook records frame for frame, and two recordings of it are the
same frames. That is tested with raw WebGL and a context created with `preserveDrawingBuffer: true`.
three.js draws through the same WebGL and is not tested itself.

## A render section

A render section is a section whose picture a program draws rather than a page. It names a
`render_command` and no `page` and no `scene`.

{/* input: src/decktalk/template/examples/motion/decktalk.toml, section 3 */}
```toml decktalk.toml
[[section]]
number = 3
chapter = "A picture from elsewhere"
render_command = ["{python}", "render/draw_bars", "{motion}", "{out}", "{landings}", "{ffmpeg}"]
hold_seconds = 1.0
```

It is narrated exactly like a page section. Its take decides its length, `cue` resolves its cues from
its words, and the voice runs straight through it. That is the difference from a clip section,
which carries its own sound and pauses the narration while it plays.

`record` writes the section's motion document and runs the command once, from the project root, with
each placeholder in it replaced.

| Placeholder | What it becomes |
|---|---|
| `{motion}` | The motion document the command reads, `build/recordings/render/NN/motion.json`. |
| `{out}` | Where the command writes the section's video, which is `build/recordings/NN.mp4`. |
| `{landings}` | Where the command may write its landings, `build/recordings/render/NN/landings.json`. |
| `{python}` | The Python this run of DeckTalk is running on. |
| `{ffmpeg}` | The ffmpeg this run encodes with. |

The motion document holds the frame, the length, every spoken word and every cue, and every second
in it is on the section clock.

```json build/recordings/render/03/motion.json
{
 "fps": 25,
 "width": 1920,
 "height": 1080,
 "seconds": 9.4,
 "frames": 235,
 "words": [{ "word": "This", "start": 0.5, "end": 0.71 }],
 "cues": { "3.1:one": 4.12, "3.1:two": 5.3, "3.1:three": 6.48 }
}
```

The numbers above show the shape and are not from a real run. A renderer keys a change on a cue at
frame `round(cue * fps)`.

The command is a program the project names, so it runs only on a machine that allows it through
`record.programs`, as [programs a project names](/requirements#programs-a-project-names) says. It
runs with your user's rights and can read every file you can, `.env` included. It runs in an empty
scratch folder rather than the project, a word that names a project file by a relative path such as
`render/draw_bars` is handed to it as that file's absolute path, and it is given the scrubbed
environment with no proxy, so a credential in your shell does not reach it. It may run for
`tools.timeout_seconds`. It fails the section when it exits with an error or writes no video. The section is recorded again when the command, the
motion document or a project file the command names changes, so editing `render/draw_bars` draws
the section again.

A machine whose `record.page_policy` is `untrusted` runs no program a project names, so a render
section is refused there with `INPUT`.

### Landings

A command may write `{landings}` as a list of lanes in the shape a page reports its own, and verify
then masks and measures them as it does a page's.

```json build/recordings/render/03/landings.json
[
 {
  "id": "bar-1",
  "kind": "motion",
  "box": { "x": 0, "y": 270, "w": 1600, "h": 135 },
  "size": { "w": 1280, "h": 135 },
  "departs": 4.12,
  "arrives": 4.52,
  "landings": [{ "cue": "3.1:one", "kind": "depart", "at": 4.12 }]
 }
]
```

| Field | What it holds |
|---|---|
| `id` | A name for the lane, which no other lane in the section shares. |
| `kind` | `motion`, `loop` or `region`. |
| `box` | The whole box the lane swept, in the recording's pixels. |
| `size` | The size of the moving thing itself, which is the area a landing is measured against. |
| `departs` and `arrives` | The first and last second the lane moved, on the section clock. |
| `landings` | Each cue the lane lands on, as its cue id, `depart` or `arrive`, and the cue's second. |

A file in any other shape fails the section. A section whose command writes no landings has every
cue skipped as `rendered`, and verify says on its stream that it measured none of them, because a
frame difference over a picture nobody described could read the picture's own motion as a reveal.

`render/draw_bars` in the example is a Python script with no dependency beyond the standard library.
It reads the motion document, composes one ffmpeg filter graph in which a bar slides in on each of
the three cues, runs the ffmpeg it was handed, and writes one lane per bar so verify measures each
departure against its word.

Remotion, a three.js scene under a frame stepper of its own, or any program that turns a JSON file
into a video fits the same contract. Wiring Remotion as a `render_command` is untested, and it is
never a dependency of DeckTalk. Remotion is free for individuals and small companies and paid above
that, so read [its licence](https://www.remotion.dev/license) before a project depends on it.

## What verify does with each

The recorder samples every declared motion, loop, region and pane clip on every frame it steps, and
the recording log keeps one **lane** for each: the box it swept, the size of the element, the first and
last second it moved, and the cues it lands on. [The lane report](/concepts/page-contract#the-lane-report)
gives its fields.

**Masks.** Every cue is measured with every lane that is alive anywhere from its control spans to
its last probe masked out of the changed share, the control share and the onset. The `Masked`
column of the verify table says how much of the frame that took. Over
`verify.motion_mask_max_percent` of the frame, 40 percent by default, raises
[`MOTION_MASK_LARGE`](/reference/findings/MOTION_MASK_LARGE), a warning that the cue was judged on
what was left.

**Landings.** A cue that is only a motion's departure or arrival, where the page reveals nothing
else, is skipped in the cue table as `motion_landing` and measured on the motion's own lane instead.
Inside the lane, frame by frame, the motion counts as moving while more than
`verify.motion_floor_percent` of its own area changes, 0.5 percent by default. Its departure is the
first moving frame from one reference lead before its cue, and its arrival is the last moving frame.
Each landing is a row in a second table, under the cue table.

| Column | JSON field | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `Section` | `section` | The section number. |
| `Motion` | `cue` | The cue id the motion lands on, or the clip and its own cue for a landing inside a pane clip. |
| `Landing` | `kind` | `depart` or `arrive`. |
| `Spoken` | `spoken` | When the cue's word is spoken in the film. |
| `Shown` | `shown` | The first or the last moving frame, or null when it never moved. |
| `Offset` | `offset_seconds` | `Shown` less `Spoken`. |

**Pane clips.** A pane clip is a lane from its cue on, and its cue is a departure measured on the
pane. The landings a clip carries beside it, in `<clip>.landings.json`, are moved onto the film's
clock from that cue and into the pane, and each is a row of the same table naming its clip.

A landing on a cue that `cues.json` marks `"verify": false` has no row. [Verify](/reference/verify#motions)
gives the whole measurement.

## On a still

`storyboard` and `check` draw stills, and a still has no clock. A loop rests in its own style, a
motion whose departure cue has fired is drawn where it ends, and a pane clip shows its first frame.

## The findings

| Code | Raised by | What happened | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| [`PAGE_MOTION_UNDECLARED`](/reference/findings/PAGE_MOTION_UNDECLARED) | `check` and `record` | Something still moves 0.46 seconds after it starts and declares no motion, loop or region. `check` reads every slide's stylesheet for it, and `record` catches anything a script starts. | Declare it as a motion or a loop, or give its frame hook a region. |
| [`PAGE_MOTION_CUE_UNKNOWN`](/reference/findings/PAGE_MOTION_CUE_UNKNOWN) | `record` | A motion or a pane clip names a cue with no second, or a motion arrives no later than it departs. | Correct the cue name, add its row to `cues.json`, or list a script's cue in `data-owns`. |
| [`PAGE_CHANGE_UNDECLARED`](/reference/findings/PAGE_CHANGE_UNDECLARED) | `record` | A timer or an animation frame of the page's own changed the picture inside no declared motion, loop, region or pane clip. | Make the change in a cue's handler, draw it from a frame hook with a region, or declare it as a motion. |
| [`PAGE_MOTION_UNFINISHED`](/reference/findings/PAGE_MOTION_UNFINISHED) | `record` | A script motion's `draw` still changes its element half a second after the arrival, which is never drawn. | Move the arrival to a later cue, or end the change by the arrival. |
| [`PAGE_REALTIME_MEDIA`](/reference/findings/PAGE_REALTIME_MEDIA) | `check` and `record` | A slide holds a `<video>` or an `<audio>`. | Play the footage in a pane with `data-clip` or as a clip section, or draw the picture in a render section. |
| [`PAGE_CLIP_UNREADABLE`](/reference/findings/PAGE_CLIP_UNREADABLE) | `record` | A pane clip names a file the origin could not decode into a picture. | Point `data-clip` at a video file the page may load, or check the `[[clip]]` that makes it. |
| [`PAGE_FRAME_THREW`](/reference/findings/PAGE_FRAME_THREW) | `record` | A frame hook threw, and it draws nothing from that frame on. | Open the browser console under `decktalk serve` and fix the hook. |
| [`MOTION_EASE_UNMEASURABLE`](/reference/findings/MOTION_EASE_UNMEASURABLE) | `check` | A `settle` or `spring` motion arrives on a cue verify would measure. | Make the ease `firm`, or mark the arrival cue `"verify": false`. |
| [`MOTION_OFF`](/reference/findings/MOTION_OFF) | `verify` | A departure or an arrival landed outside the offset limit of its word, or a departure never moved. | Read the offset in the message, and look at the cue's phrase as you would for `CUE_OFF`. |
| [`MOTION_NO_EDGE`](/reference/findings/MOTION_NO_EDGE) | `verify` | An arrival came to rest before its word by more than the limit, or never moved near it. | Make the ease `firm`. |
| [`MOTION_MASK_LARGE`](/reference/findings/MOTION_MASK_LARGE) | `verify` | Declared motion covered over 40 percent of the frame around a cue. | Make the moving thing smaller or move the cue away from it, so the reveal is judged on more of the picture. |
