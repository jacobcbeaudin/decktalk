---
title: Accessible video
description: What a build writes for a viewer who cannot see or cannot hear the film, what is yours to write, and which findings hold each of them honest.
---

Course platforms, company intranets and public sector sites often require captions and a transcript
by law. A DeckTalk build writes most of what that takes without being asked, because it already knows
the spoken words, where each one falls and what appeared on which second.

The parts it cannot write are the ones only you know: what a reveal meant, and what a picture is of.
Each of those has a judgement behind it, so a description you did not write is reported rather than
missed. This page is the whole of both halves.

## What every build writes

Everything below lands in `build/final/`, beside the film, and `<name>` is `[project] name`.

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `<name>.srt` and `<name>.vtt` | The captions, in SubRip and WebVTT, cut from the words the voice really said and lent the script's own punctuation and case. |
| `<name>.chapters.txt` | The chapter markers, one per section, named by the section's `chapter` and falling back to its script heading. |
| `<name>-transcript.html` | The transcript page, which is the media alternative for a viewer who cannot see the film. |
| `<name>-poster.png` | The film's opening slide with every one of its reveals fired, drawn by the page rather than taken from the mp4. |

The chapters are muxed into the mp4 as well, so a player shows them without the text file, and the
text file is there for a platform that wants to be handed one.

### One language, stated once

`[project] language` is the language tag of the narration, and `en` is the default. The transcript
page takes it as its `lang` attribute, and the mp4's audio and video streams are tagged with its
three-letter form, which is the code the container takes rather than the tag a page carries. One
value reaches both, so a player naming the audio and a reader opening the transcript cannot be told
two different things.

## Captions carry the whole soundtrack

Captions owe the audio and not only the dialogue. A viewer who cannot hear the ball bounce has to be
told that it bounced.

Every cued sound takes a `caption` string, and the build writes it into the caption files as a cue of
its own, wrapped in square brackets.

```toml decktalk.toml
[[mix.effects]]
file = "build/soundscape/bounce.mp3"
section = 2
cue = "2.1:ball"
caption = "ball bounces"
```

A sound with no `caption` is in the film and in no caption file, and `assemble` says so on every run,
naming the file, the cue and the section. The frozen list of codes holds none that means this, so it
is a sentence on the stream rather than a code that means something else.
[Put a sound effect on a cue](/guides/effects-on-a-cue) is how a sound gets cued in the first place.

A sound's caption stays on screen for one second. Two captions are never on screen at once, because a
player draws overlapping cues twice or drops one of them, so a sound that lands inside a speech cue
joins that caption as a line of its own and a sound that lands in the short gap before one joins the
cue after it. Only a sound with a whole second to itself keeps a cue of its own.

A clip section captions its own speech when it names a words file with `words`, and nothing captions
it otherwise, because the captions are built from the narration's word times and a clip's speech is
not in the narration. [Add a clip section](/guides/clip-section) has the file's shape.

## The transcript, and the words a reveal is described in

The transcript page carries, for every chapter in the order it plays: a heading, the span in the
finished film, the spoken text of each section under it, the speech inside a clip that names a words
file, and a list of that chapter's described reveals with the second each one fired.

What it cannot carry on its own is what a reveal meant. Three attributes are where you write that,
and each one is read by the runtime and reported into the recording log, which is what the transcript
is built from.

| Attribute | Where | What it carries |
|---|---|---|
| `data-describe` | an element or a slide | The subject of the reveal. The runtime gives it a verb for each moment, so the phrase is a subject rather than a sentence. An empty phrase means the reveal is decorative. |
| `data-describe-class` | an element or a container | What each class change means, written as `moment:phrase` pairs separated by a vertical bar. It is the partner of `data-class`. |
| `data-describe-out` | an element | The departure sentence, for the case where a thing leaving means something of its own. |

```html
<figure data-in="error" data-describe="the error curve, falling steeply and then flattening">
```

```html
<li data-class="highlight:lit" data-describe-class="highlight:the second row is marked as the answer">
```

Describe the reveals that carry meaning. A decorative flourish needs no phrase, and an empty
`data-describe` is how you say so on purpose rather than by leaving the attribute off. A description
of every flourish only makes the transcript harder to read.

The starter writes a phrase on every cued element, so a first project's transcript already reads end
to end. [The page contract](/concepts/page-contract) is every attribute in context, and
`decktalk schema page` prints all twenty-four with their values, their defaults and their codes.

## What holds each of them honest

Every promise on this page has a judgement behind it, so nothing here depends on remembering to look.

| What is missing | Code | Certainty | Reached by |
|---|---|---|---|
| An element changes the picture and describes nothing | [`PAGE_NO_DESCRIPTION`](/reference/findings/PAGE_NO_DESCRIPTION) | certain | `decktalk check`, from the page's own catalog |
| `data-class` names a class with no phrase in `data-describe-class` | [`PAGE_CLASS_UNDESCRIBED`](/reference/findings/PAGE_CLASS_UNDESCRIBED) | certain | any command that opens the page, reported by the runtime |
| A class change declares no reduced form, so a reduced render would animate anyway | [`PAGE_CLASS_NOT_REDUCED`](/reference/findings/PAGE_CLASS_NOT_REDUCED) | certain | any command that opens the page, reported by the runtime |
| The mixed film misses the loudness it was mastered to | [`MIX_LOUDNESS`](/reference/findings/MIX_LOUDNESS) | uncertain | `decktalk assemble`, measured on the finished mix |
| A reveal did not land on the word it was cued to | [`CUE_OFF`](/reference/findings/CUE_OFF) | certain | `decktalk verify`, measured on the finished film |
| A reveal cannot be told from the frame before it | [`CUE_NO_CHANGE`](/reference/findings/CUE_NO_CHANGE) | certain | `decktalk verify` and `decktalk check` |

`--fail-on certain` is the default, so the certain ones fail a run already. `--fail-on any` adds the
uncertain ones, which is how a project says it wants the second opinions too, and `--allow CODE`
carries one code past the threshold and repeats.

A cue that landed on the wrong word is an accessibility failure and not only a timing one: a viewer
reading the captions meets the reveal at the wrong moment, and a viewer reading the transcript reads
the wrong second beside it.

## The loudness the mix is mastered to

A film that is quieter than everything else on a platform is a film a hard-of-hearing viewer turns up
and then gets startled by. The mix is therefore mastered rather than left where the layers landed.

`assemble` measures the whole soundtrack, applies one gain to reach
[`[mix.loudness] target_lufs`](/reference/configuration#mix-loudness), runs a true-peak limiter at
`true_peak_max_dbtp`, and measures the result again. A plain gain keeps the mix's dynamics intact and
the limiter touches only the peaks that would cross the ceiling, and it runs oversampled so that
inter-sample peaks are caught, which is what a true-peak ceiling promises.

| Key | Default | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| `target_lufs` | `-16.0` | The programme loudness the film is normalised to. |
| `true_peak_max_dbtp` | `-1.5` | The highest true peak the normalised mix may reach. |
| `range_max_lu` | `11.0` | The widest loudness range the normalised mix may hold. |

The second measurement is reported, and a miss is `MIX_LOUDNESS`. It is uncertain, because the pass
reports what it could not fix and a reader decides: a film whose range is wide because the speech is
quiet under loud music is a different problem from one whose target was set wrong.

## Reduced motion

Some viewers ask their own machine for less motion, and some cannot watch a film that moves at all.
DeckTalk renders that film rather than asking the viewer's player to.

No flag duplicates a settings key, so both of these are `--set` on any command.

```console
decktalk build --set motion.reduce=true --no-voice
```

- `motion.reduce=true` renders every slide as its reduced-motion variant. Chromium is asked for
  `prefers-reduced-motion`, which is a media feature rather than an attribute, so the film is exactly
  what a person who asked their own machine for less motion would see in a preview. A class change
  whose stylesheet animates anyway is reported as `PAGE_CLASS_NOT_REDUCED`, because a page can
  declare reduced motion and then not honour it.
- `motion.scale=N` multiplies every declared motion span and duration in the deck, from 0.25 to 4.
  It is put on `:root` as `--dt-motion-scale`, which the deck's own stylesheet reads. A span slowed
  past the half second a cue may still move is clamped at that ceiling rather than obeyed, because a
  span that covers its own cue cannot be measured, and a span that overruns it is
  [`PAGE_MOTION_OVERRUN`](/reference/findings/PAGE_MOTION_OVERRUN).

Both keys are part of what a recording is keyed on, so a reduced build never reuses a full-motion
recording and the calmer film is a second build rather than a second project. The film is written to
the same name, so copy the one you want to keep out of `build/final/` before you build the other, or
give the calm build its own `[project] build` directory.

## What the author still owes

- **Give every image its alt text.** A slide image is ordinary HTML, and nothing writes its `alt`
  attribute for you.
- **Change lightness, not only hue.** `decktalk verify` measures luma alone, so a reveal that changes
  colour without changing lightness fails the check and also fails a viewer with low vision. The two
  failures have one fix.
- **Say the thing you show.** A reveal the narration never names has no word to start on and no place
  in the transcript, which is the same rule [Writing for the ear](/guides/writing-for-the-ear) gives
  for a different reason.
- **Keep what matters out of the bottom of the frame.** A player draws captions across the bottom of
  the picture, and DeckTalk does not measure that, so it is yours to watch for.

## How to check it

<Steps>
  <Step title="Judge the pages before you build">
    ```console
    decktalk check --fail-on any
    ```

    `check` produces nothing and opens the pages, so this is where a missing description, an
    undescribed class change and a class that animates under reduced motion are met. `--fail-on any`
    adds the uncertain findings to the ones that already fail.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Measure the finished film">
    ```console
    decktalk verify
    ```

    Every section start, every cut, every seam and every cue landing is measured against the clock
    the earlier stages promised. A reveal that did not land on its word is a reveal a caption reader
    meets at the wrong moment.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Read the transcript with the film closed">
    Open `build/final/<name>-transcript.html` and read it on its own. A chapter whose list of reveals
    is short or missing is a chapter whose page needs its phrases written.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Watch it with the sound off">
    Play the film with the captions on and the sound off. Anything you learn only from the audio is a
    caption line that is not there.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Publishing

Serve the film from a plain `<video>` element with a `<track>` for the captions, so the captions and
the chapters stay under your control rather than a third-party player's. Put the poster on the
element, link the transcript page next to the film, and give that link text that says what it is
rather than the word "transcript" on its own.
