---
title: Play a clip
description: Play a video of your own as one section of the film, and cut a piece of the finished film into a video of its own.
---

A clip is one thing in DeckTalk, which is a short video file. The word is used two ways on this page
and means the same thing each time.

- A `[[section]]` with `clip = "media/intro.mp4"` plays a video file you supply, with its own audio,
  as one section of the film. The voice says nothing there, because the clip carries its own sound.
- `decktalk clip` writes a video file cut from a section of the finished film, with its own audio and
  its own words file, so a piece of the film can be played again or handed to somebody else.

One noun names a short video file whoever made it, so a clip you supply, a film DeckTalk built and a
clip DeckTalk cuts are the same kind of thing, and a clip section plays any of them the same way.

## A section that plays your own video

A clip section names its file and nothing else about the picture. It has no `page` and no `scene`,
`cues.json` says nothing about it, and `script.md` needs no heading for it.

```toml decktalk.toml
[[section]]
number = 4
chapter = "On camera"
clip = "media/intro.mp4"          # your video, with its own audio
slate_seconds = 4                 # how long the slate plays while the file is missing
```

| Key | Default | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `number` | required | Where the section sits in the film. |
| `clip` | required | The video file, relative to the project. |
| `chapter` | the script heading, else `Section N` | The chapter marker's title. |
| `slate_seconds` | `5.0` | How long the titled slate plays while the file is not there. |
| `optional` | `false` | Says this section is allowed to play its slate. |
| `seamless` | `false` | Says the clip opens on the previous section's last picture, which `verify` measures. |

A clip that is not the project's frame size is scaled to fit and padded with black, so a portrait
phone video plays in a letterbox rather than distorted. A clip with no audio track plays silent, and
`assemble` says so on the stream.

### While the file is not there

A clip section whose file is missing plays a titled slate for `slate_seconds`, so a project can carry
the section before the video exists and everyone can watch the film with a hole labelled rather than
a hole. The slate carries the section number, the line `Your clip goes here`, and the path to drop the
file at. It is kept under `build/stills/` by everything it shows, so a renamed chapter or a new colour
draws it again. It is drawn on the colour
[`[video] slate_color`](/reference/configuration#video) names, and `[mix] slate` points at an image of
your own to use in its place.

Nothing about a slate is silent about itself: `decktalk status` reports the missing file as an error,
[`FILE_MISSING`](/reference/findings/FILE_MISSING), naming the section and the path, and a build writes
the film with the slate in it and then stops before `verify`. A section marked `optional` is the one
exception, because it has said the slate is allowed, so its film is measured and the build exits 0.

### Putting one between two page sections

A section's number is its place in the film, so adding a clip in the middle renumbers what follows it.

1. In `decktalk.toml`, add 1 to the `number` of every section after the new clip, from the last one
   upward, then add the clip section's own table.
2. In `script.md`, renumber the same headings. The clip section needs no heading of its own, and a
   heading there is never voiced, because the voice reads every section that does not play a clip.
3. In `cues.json`, rename each later section key, from the last one upward. The [cue id](/concepts/cues#the-cue-id)s do not
   move, because they name slides, and the slides of the page are untouched by a renumbering of the
   film.
4. Run `decktalk build`.

A renumbered section is voiced again by nothing. A take is named by the digest of what was sent to
the voice, and the section number is not one of those things, so the take is found by its content and
the run buys nothing. [Rebuild one section](/guides/rebuild-one-section) is the whole of that rule.

## How a clip splits the narration

The joined narration holds the spoken sections back to back with no gap, and the film does not. A
clip between two page sections pauses the narration, and the next page section resumes it on its own
first frame. So the track plays in runs of consecutive page sections, and each run starts where its
first section starts in the film.

One rule places the track, and the captions and the cut check read it too, so all three agree about
where a moment of the narration plays.

- A page section's `hold_seconds` splits the narration exactly as a clip does, because the hold is
  picture with no speech under it.
- The clip's own audio joins the mix at its section start, matched to the narration's loudness,
  trimmed to its picture and faded over twenty milliseconds at each edge, so a cut into it never
  clicks.
- The music ducks under the whole of a clip, because a clip's dialogue is its own. [Sound](/concepts/sound)
  is the whole mix.
- A clip section gets its own chapter marker, unless the section before it carries the same
  `chapter`, in which case the two share one.
- The narration does not depend on how long a clip runs. After you replace a clip file, run
  `decktalk assemble` and then `decktalk verify`, and nothing is voiced or recorded.

## Play a film DeckTalk built

A film DeckTalk built writes its words beside itself, as `build/final/<name>.words.json`: every word
the film says, as its captions show it, with its voice and its turn. Copy the film and that file into
the project under one name, such as `media/larkbill.mp4` and `media/larkbill.words.json`, and a clip
section that plays the film has its speech captioned on this film's clock.

## Caption the speech inside a clip

The captions are built from the narration's word times, so a clip's own speech is captioned from
the words file beside the clip, `<clip>.words.json`, and by nothing when there is none. A words file
is one object with a `words` list, and each word carries its span in seconds after the clip starts.

```json media/intro.words.json
{
  "words": [
    { "word": "Watch", "start": 0.15, "end": 0.42 },
    { "word": "it.", "start": 0.48, "end": 0.71 }
  ]
}
```

Give each word its own punctuation and case, because the captions break where the sentences end. Save
it beside the clip under the clip's name, as `media/intro.words.json` beside `media/intro.mp4`, and
run `decktalk assemble`. `decktalk clip` writes this file beside every clip it cuts, and every build
beside its film.

- A word that starts after the clip's picture ends is left out.
- A slate has no captions, and neither has a clip with no audio track.
- A clip with no words file beside it plays with no captions.
- The transcript page carries the same words as one paragraph, so a reader is not left with a silent
  gap where the clip is.

A take's own `<digest>.words.json` in `takes/` has this shape, and so does the file
`decktalk clip` writes beside every clip it cuts. A word may also carry `voice` and `turn`, which
`decktalk clip` and a build's `<name>.words.json` write: a caption of it carries them as
`data-voice` and `data-turn` in the captioned copy, so a page sets it by its voice, and no caption
file names them.

## Cut a clip out of the film

`decktalk clip` cuts a span of one built page or render section into its own file. The picture is a run of whole
frames of `build/sections/NN.mp4` and the sound is the finished film's over the same span, so a clip
is a piece of the film rather than a second render of it: it carries the voice, the effects and the
score, as the film mixed them.

The clock is the section's own. Zero is where the section starts, and the lead of silence before its
first word is part of the span like anything else.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Find the span">
    `decktalk words` is the one command that maps script text to seconds, and it is the clock
    `--start` and `--end` are written against.

    ```console
    decktalk words --section 1
    ```

    Each row is one word with its start and its end, in seconds after the section starts.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Cut it">
    ```console
    decktalk clip --section 1 --start 0.48 --end 1.92 --hold-seconds 0.5 --out media/open-line.mp4
    ```

    | Flag | Default | What it does |
    |---|---|---|
    | `--section N` | required | The one page or render section to cut from. It names exactly one section. |
    | `--start SECONDS` | `0` | Where the clip starts on that section's clock. |
    | `--end SECONDS` | required | Where the clip ends, after `--start`. |
    | `--out FILE` | `clip-N.mp4` | Where to write the clip. |
    | `--gain-db DB` | `0` | Lifts or cuts the clip's audio, so a clip can match the film it is played beside. |
    | `--hold-seconds SECONDS` | `0` | Holds the last frame this long, in silence, after the span, rounded to whole frames. |

    A `clip` without `--end` is refused as a [`USAGE`](/reference/errors/USAGE) error, which exits 2,
    before anything is read.

    The span is rounded outward to whole frames, because a clip that began part way through a frame
    would play its first frame twice. At the default twenty-five frames a second, that is a fortieth
    of a second either way.

    Two files are written: the clip, and `<name>.words.json` beside it, holding every word wholly
    inside the span in seconds after the clip starts. A word the span cuts in two is left out and
    said aloud on the stream, so move `--end` into the silence beside the word to keep it.

    {/* sample: clip, decktalk clip --section 1 --start 0.48 --end 1.92 --hold-seconds 0.5 --out media/open-line.mp4, on a starter built with --no-spend */}
    ```text
    Section 1 carries placeholder narration, so every word time in this clip is an estimate.
    Cut media/open-line.mp4, 1.9 seconds of section 1.
    ```

    A span that cuts a word adds one line before those, such as `The span from 0.48s to 1.68s cuts
    the word 'DeckTalk.' in two, so the words file leaves it out.`
  </Step>

  <Step title="Play it in a clip section">
    A clip section names the clip, and its captions come from the words file the command wrote
    beside it.

    ```toml decktalk.toml
    [[section]]
    number = 4
    chapter = "The opening line again"
    clip = "media/open-line.mp4"
    ```

    Then run `decktalk assemble` and `decktalk verify`.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The JSON is one flat object, so a caller reads what was written without parsing a line.

{/* sample: clip-json, decktalk clip --section 1 --start 0.48 --end 1.92 --hold-seconds 0.5 --out media/open-line.mp4 --json, on a starter built with --no-spend */}
```json
{
  "schema": 1,
  "ok": true,
  "findings": [],
  "error": null,
  "run": "78d56eab7905",
  "written": ["media/open-line.mp4", "media/open-line.words.json"],
  "section": 1,
  "file": "media/open-line.mp4",
  "words_file": "media/open-line.words.json",
  "start_seconds": 0.48,
  "end_seconds": 1.92,
  "seconds": 1.92,
  "hold_seconds": 0.48,
  "gain_db": 0.0,
  "estimated": true
}
```

`seconds` is the whole clip, the span and the hold together, and `hold_seconds` is the hold as whole
frames.

`estimated` is true when the section carries placeholder narration, which is the one fact that
decides whether the word times in the file beside the clip can be trusted.

### What it refuses, and why

`clip` reads two things the pipeline wrote, so it refuses rather than guessing when either is not
there. Each refusal names the command that makes what is missing.

| The refusal | What to do |
|---|---|
| The section has no section video | Run `decktalk assemble` first. |
| The section has no take, or the take file is gone | Run `decktalk narrate`, or `decktalk narrate --no-spend` to spend nothing. |
| The section plays a clip the project already has | Cut from a page or render section, which is one DeckTalk recorded. There is nothing to cut out of a file you supplied. |
| The section has no place in a finished film at `build/final/<name>.mp4` | Run `decktalk assemble` first, because a clip's sound is cut from the film. |
| The span ends before it starts, or starts before zero | Write `--start` at 0 or later and `--end` after it. |
| The span holds no whole frame | Make it at least one frame long. |
| The span ends past the section | Write `--end` at the section's own length or earlier, which the refusal states. |
| `--out` names the section video itself | Write the clip somewhere else, such as under `media/`. |
