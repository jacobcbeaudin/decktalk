---
title: Writing for the ear
description: Write a script the voice can say, and a cue phrase the spoken words can match.
---

The script is the clock. `decktalk narrate` turns `script.md` into spoken takes with a word clock,
and `decktalk cue` turns each phrase in `cues.json` into a second on its section's clock. Nothing on
a deck page writes a second, so the only way to move a reveal is to change the words it waits for or
to nudge it off them.

This guide is about the two files that decide that timing: the sentences in `script.md` and the
phrase each row of `cues.json` names. [Design a slide](/guides/design-a-slide) is about what the
picture does once the second is known.

## Write the script before the slides

Write the whole script, then read it aloud once. The words that carry the argument are the words the
reveals start on, so you need them before you can decide what appears. A slide drawn first usually
needs a sentence nobody would say.

Give each idea one reveal and each reveal one sentence. A lesson reads best at about one reveal every
ten to fifteen seconds. Three reveals in one breath read as an animation rather than an explanation,
and a minute with no reveal leaves the viewer nothing new to look at.

## Write numbers and symbols the way you want them said

The voice reads what it is given, and a cue phrase is matched against the words it said. So "41" is
a guess about how the voice pronounces two digits, and no phrase you write can reliably match that
guess. Write the words instead.

| Write this in the script | Not this | Show this on the slide |
|---|---|---|
| forty one | 41 | `41` |
| four hundred billion | 400 billion | `400 billion knobs` |
| version two point three | v2.3 | `v2.3` |
| two x | 2x | `2x` |
| decktalk dot AI | decktalk.ai | `decktalk.ai` |
| x bar equals the mean of x | `\bar{x}` | the typeset formula |

The starter's script says "Make your own at decktalk dot AI" while its end card shows `decktalk.ai`,
and the lesson example says "four hundred billion" while its slide shows `400 billion knobs`. The
number on screen and the number in the ear are the same figure written twice, once for each sense.

### The TAKE_SPOKEN_SYMBOL finding

`decktalk check` scans the spoken text of every section and raises
[`TAKE_SPOKEN_SYMBOL`](/reference/findings/TAKE_SPOKEN_SYMBOL) for a section whose words hold a digit
or one of the characters `$ € £ % & @ # * / + = < > ~ ^ |`. The finding names the section and lists
the first five offending words.

```console
decktalk check --no-pages
```

`--no-pages` judges the written files with no browser at all, which is the fastest way to read this
one finding. The finding is uncertain, because "41" may be exactly what you want the voice to try, so
it does not fail a run under the default `--fail-on certain`. Two things clear it:

- **Rewrite the word.** Replace the digit or the symbol with the words the voice should say. This is
  the fix in almost every case, because it is also the only way a cue phrase can match.
- **Accept it deliberately.** Pass `--allow TAKE_SPOKEN_SYMBOL` on the commands that would otherwise
  report it, and `--fail-on any` when you want every uncertain finding to fail a run instead.

A respelling meant to steady the voice becomes the spoken word, the cue phrase and the caption
together, so say so in the project when you respell a name.

## The beat marker and the timed pause

A short silence before a reveal makes the reveal an event. Bracketed directions are the only way a
script asks for one, and they are never spoken.

| Direction | What the voice receives | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| `[beat]` | a dash appended to the paragraph before it | Use it freely, before any reveal that should land on its own. |
| `[pause 4]` | a `<break time="4s" />` tag | Use it where the viewer should stop and think, and use it rarely. |
| Any other bracketed note, on a line of its own | nothing, and a beat after the paragraph before it | Use it as a note to yourself, such as which scene a section plays. |

{/* input: src/decktalk/template/starter/script.md, section 1 */}
```md script.md
## 1. Open

[Scene 1. The title and the count of files, then the two files you write.]

This is DeckTalk. [beat] Four files sit in one folder, and you write two of them. [beat]
You write what you will say, [beat] and you write the slides that go with it. [beat]
Then every picture waits for its own word.
```

Only a timed pause sends a break tag, because frequent break tags unsettle the voice. Back-to-back
directions do not add up and the longest one wins, and a direction before the first paragraph of a
section has nothing to pause after, so it is dropped.

### What a script may not hold

Inside a paragraph, `[beat]` and `[pause N]` are the only bracketed spans allowed, along with an
ordinary Markdown link. Any other bracketed note inside a paragraph becomes a pause nobody asked for,
so `decktalk narrate` refuses it and `decktalk check` reports it as
[`TAKE_PLACEHOLDER`](/reference/findings/TAKE_PLACEHOLDER) with the line it sits on. A brace and an
HTML comment are refused for the opposite reason: the voice reads both of them out.

A bracketed word in capitals, such as `[CUSTOMER_COUNT]`, is an open placeholder. Write one wherever
you have a claim nobody has confirmed, because a voiced run would otherwise buy the claim as it
stands. `TAKE_PLACEHOLDER` is certain, so it fails a run until the placeholder is filled.

[script.md](/reference/script-md) lists every rule about the file.

## Choose a phrase the voice really says

A cue row names a wire id and a phrase. The wire id is the slide and the local name the page wrote,
joined by a colon, and the phrase is a word or a short phrase from that section of `script.md`.

{/* input: src/decktalk/template/examples/lesson/cues.json, section 1 */}
```json cues.json
{ "cue": "1.4:downhill", "on": "steps downhill", "occurrence": 1 }
```

The script says "So it steps downhill, again and again, and the steps shrink as the ground flattens",
and the steps on the slide arrive as the word "steps" is said.

Four rules decide whether a phrase works.

1. **It has to be spoken.** The match is against the words of that section alone, and a heading, a
   bracketed direction and a word in another section are all invisible to it.
2. **It starts on the word that names the thing.** A cue time is the start of the phrase's first
   word, so a phrase beginning "and then" lands the picture on nothing. Among the starter's phrases
   are `This is DeckTalk`, `what you will say`, `the slides`, `the formula` and `in code`.
3. **It is two or three words long.** A single common word such as "the", "one" or "it" matches early
   and lands on the wrong sentence.
4. **It is copied, not retyped.** The match drops case and every character but a letter, a digit and
   an apostrophe, so `Four files` matches "four files" and `project` does not match "project's".

Set `"case_sensitive": true` on a row when the case really is the difference between two matches.

A phrase that is never spoken is [`CUE_UNRESOLVED`](/reference/findings/CUE_UNRESOLVED), which is
certain, because there is no second to put the picture at. Fix it by quoting the script again, not by
moving the limit.

### occurrence, when the phrase is not unique

A repeated phrase matches every run of it, and the first match is usually too early. `occurrence`
picks which one, counting from 1.

```json cues.json
{ "cue": "3.2:zero", "on": "Zero", "occurrence": 2 }
```

That row lands on the second time section 3 says "zero". Prefer changing the phrase to naming an
occurrence, because an occurrence is a count a later edit to the script silently changes. Every row
of the lesson example names its occurrence anyway, so a phrase the script repeats later can never
move a picture by accident.

## offset, which moves a cue off the start of its word

`offset` adds seconds to the resolved second. A positive value moves the cue later and a negative
value moves it earlier.

```json cues.json
{ "cue": "1.4:downhill", "on": "steps downhill", "offset": 0.2 }
```

- **A positive offset of 0.1 to 0.2 seconds.** The reveal lands just after the word starts, while the
  word is still being said, which reads as the narrator pointing at something.
- **A small negative offset.** Use it only where the viewer has to read along, such as a line of code
  the voice reads out. The text must be on screen before its first word is spoken.

Keep an offset under half a second. A reveal that needs more than that belongs on a different word,
and the offset is hiding the fact that the phrase is wrong. The starter writes no offset at all, so
every reveal in a new project starts on its word.

The resolved second carries the offset inside it, and `build/cue-times.json` records the offset
separately so you can see what the phrase alone would have given.

## $start and $end

Two anchors stand in for a phrase. `$start` is the section start, which is zero on its clock, and
`$end` is the end of the section's last spoken word. Give the last reveal of a section `$end` and it
lands as the voice stops.

```json cues.json
{ "cue": "3.4:end", "on": "$end", "offset": 0.3 }
```

`$end` is the end of speech and not the end of the section's audio, so silence after the last word
belongs to `hold_seconds` on that `[[section]]` instead. [cues.json](/reference/cues-json) lists
every key a row may carry.

## Prove it before you spend anything

Two commands read the clock without buying a take.

```console
decktalk words --section 1
decktalk check
```

`decktalk words` prints every spoken word with its start and its end, which is the clock every phrase
is written against. Before a voiced run the words are estimated from the text, and after one they are
the times the voice really took.

`decktalk check` judges `script.md`, `cues.json` and the pages together, prices what a voiced build
would cost and writes `build/storyboard.html`. It reports a moment on the page that no row names as
[`CUE_MISSING`](/reference/findings/CUE_MISSING), a row the page does not declare as
[`CUE_UNKNOWN`](/reference/findings/CUE_UNKNOWN), and two cues close enough together to read as one
as [`CUE_OVERLAP`](/reference/findings/CUE_OVERLAP). `decktalk check --fix` applies every safe fix,
and the fix for `CUE_MISSING` adds the row with an empty `on` for you to write the phrase into.
